# Taking the chart away with you

The atlas can put the chart you are looking at onto a sheet of
paper. **File → Export Chart Sheet…** asks three things and writes
one file.

Everything on the sheet is the chart you had on screen: the same
centre, the same field, the same stars and deep-sky objects, the
same grid, the same constellation figures and boundaries, and
whichever of the observer's lines and the ecliptic you had switched
on. Nothing is added and nothing is left out.

## The three formats

**SVG** — a drawing you can open and change. Every line is a line and
every label is text, so you can retype a name, delete a label that is
in your way, change a colour, or add your own note, in Inkscape or
any other vector editor. Ink and labels carry their own classes, so
an editor can select one kind at a time. It is the smallest of the
three.

**PDF** — a page you can print. It states its own physical size, so
the printer's dialog will offer it at that size rather than guessing.
Its labels are drawn as shapes rather than as letters, which means
they cannot be edited or searched — the reason is below.

**PNG** — a picture you can send. For a message, a mailing list or a
club page, where a drawing file would be a nuisance to open. It says
what physical size it is meant to be, so printing it gives you the
sheet rather than whatever fills the page.

If you are not sure: **PDF to print, SVG to work on, PNG to send.**

## The paper

**A4** or **US Letter**, landscape, with half-inch (12.7 mm) margins.
Both are offered because guessing from where your computer thinks it
is would be guessing.

| | chart area |
|---|---|
| A4 | 271.6 × 184.6 mm |
| US Letter | 254.0 × 190.5 mm |

The chart is drawn for the paper, not squeezed into it: an A4 sheet
and a Letter sheet of the same chart show slightly different amounts
of sky, because they are different shapes.

## Resolution

Only PNG has one, so the choice is greyed out for the other two.
**300 dots per inch** is the default and prints well. 150 makes a
smaller file for a screen; 600 makes a large one for a printer you
know is worth it.

## The working selection

If you have marked objects while observing, those rings and crosses
are yours and the sheet leaves them out unless you ask for them.
A sheet outlives the evening that made it, and a chart handed to
somebody else carrying your half-finished list is telling them
something that is not about the sky. The switch is there when you
want it.

## Why the PDF cannot be edited

The chart writes Greek letters — α, β, γ — and the degree and minute
signs. The fonts every PDF reader is guaranteed to have cannot spell
those, and a PDF that carried its own font would be a much larger
file and a licence question. So the labels are drawn as their shapes.
They print exactly right and cannot be retyped.

The SVG has the same labels as text. If you want to change a word,
that is the file to open.

## What a sheet says about itself

Each file carries its centre, its field width, its coordinate frame,
its magnitude limit, the paper it was made for, and which version of
JUranometria drew it. It carries nothing about you: no file paths, no
user name, no folder. A sheet is safe to send to strangers.

Nothing in a sheet is fetched from anywhere. Opened on a machine with
no network, on a different continent, in ten years, it is the same
chart.

## Printing

Open the PDF and print it. In the print dialog, choose **actual
size** — not "fit to page", which is usually the default and will
shrink the sheet by a few per cent to fit inside your printer's own
unprintable margin. The chart is already inside a half-inch margin
for exactly that reason.
