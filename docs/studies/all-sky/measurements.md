# All-sky projection study (#686) - measurements

Generated by `make all-sky-study` (`juranometria.tool.allsky.AllSkyStudyMain`). Geometry and verdicts only; ink counts and boxes are in `platform.md`. Every page is production ink drawn through the #686 study door (`Projections.drawnForStudy`, removed by #687) except the outline, the ecliptic, the equator line and the horizon, which production cannot draw on a seamed map and the study draws in production's strokes (`AllSkyStudyInk`); every page with study ink says so in its corner.

## 1. Sources of the mathematics

The formulas are Wolfram MathWorld's transcriptions (read 2026-11-08), each attributed by MathWorld to J. P. Snyder, *Map Projections - A Working Manual*, USGS Professional Paper 1395 (1987): Hammer-Aitoff eqs (1)-(5) (Snyder pp. 182, 185), Mollweide eqs (1)-(3), (7)-(9) (pp. 249-252), cylindrical equidistant eqs (1)-(4) with standard parallel 0 (pp. 90-91). The USGS copy refused an automated fetch, so the page numbers are MathWorld's and every formula is held by `AllSkyGeometryTest` instead: round trip over the whole sphere, the Mollweide auxiliary equation's residual, equal area by the Jacobian, and the outline. MathWorld's Mollweide first guess, eq (6), is not used: it gives 1.0472 where the root is 1.2672 at 45 degrees; the study solves the auxiliary angle by a bracketed Newton iteration of its own.

## 4. Handedness and the orientation sentence

The maps are drawn as the sky is seen from inside the sphere, as every page of the atlas is: celestial east to the left of north. Held by computation on the plane (x positive east is drawn leftward by the production mapping):

| form | orientation | 10 deg of increasing map longitude (right ascension, or ecliptic longitude) from centre lands | 10 deg of map latitude north lands | tilt of celestial north at the centre (worst central longitude) | widest tilt of local north from page-up over the map |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hammer | equatorial | left | up | 0.0 deg | 84 deg |
| Hammer | ecliptic | left | up | 23.4 deg | 158 deg |
| Mollweide | equatorial | left | up | 0.0 deg | 80 deg |
| Mollweide | ecliptic | left | up | 19.4 deg | 159 deg |
| equirectangular | equatorial | left | up | 0.0 deg | 0 deg |
| equirectangular | ecliptic | left | up | 23.4 deg | 180 deg |

So the production title's *Celestial north up · celestial east left* is exact at the centre of an equatorial map and nowhere else on any candidate. On an ecliptic-horizontal map it is not exact even at the centre: there the up direction is *ecliptic* north, and celestial north leans from it by up to the obliquity, depending on the central longitude. Toward the edges of either orientation local north turns far from page-up. An ecliptic map therefore needs its own orientation sentence, and any map needs one that says it is true at the centre. This is the map's own orientation statement; it claims nothing about #697.

The ruled sentence (R-12) is the one the table's first column measures on the ecliptic candidates: *ecliptic north up · ecliptic longitude increases left at centre* (nb: *ekliptisk nord opp · ekliptisk lengde øker mot venstre i midten*).

## 9. The winding horizon and Subdue below horizon

The mathematical horizon is a great circle; on these maps it is a closed winding curve. The seam is half a meridian, so every great circle not through the map's poles crosses it exactly once - the horizon, the ecliptic and the equator are each cut once on every page. With Subdue below horizon on, the part of the map below the horizon is quieted. That is half the sky by construction and, it turns out, exactly half of every candidate's area too: the two halves are antipodal, and each form's area scale depends only on the size of the latitude. What differs is where the quieted half lies and how much the rectangle inflates it toward its poles. Central meridian: observer's meridian.
