# Ecliptic-longitude ticks and a solar-longitude marker: the study (#719)

Road #726, issue #719. Generated by `make ecliptic-longitude-study` (`juranometria.tool.EclipticLongitudeStudyMain`). **A study and a mock:** every page is the application's own chart, composed as the application composes it, with the ecliptic shown; the graduation and the marker are drawn over the painted page by the study tool, from the page's own projection. No production code draws them. Positions are the production services'; the four seasonal instants are the cited fixture `docs/studies/solar-system/seasons-2026.txt` (IMCCE). Which ticks lie on a page is the sky's and the projection's and is stated here; whether a figure found a clean box depends on the fonts in front of the rasteriser and is this machine's record in `platform.md`.

The mock's rules, as page pixels: a stroke across the ecliptic 3 either side of it (the meridian graduation's length, #515), in the ecliptic's own ink, subdued below the horizon as the line is; a stroke that carries a figure is 6 either side; a figure in the grid-label font, 3 clear of the stroke's end, on the ecliptic's north side or, that refused, its south side, written only where the page as painted is all but bare under its box. The page's own writer places against boxes and catalogue ink, not pixels, so the mock's refusals are an estimate and are this machine's record.

## 2. The whole sky's ruler is not uniform

The whole-sky projection keeps areas, not distances along the ecliptic. On `whole-2-scale.png` two neighbouring strokes 10 degrees apart stand between 40.9 and 55.5 pixels apart: widest at the centre, closest at the two ends. A reader can count strokes anywhere on the ruler; a reader cannot interpolate between two figures by eye with the same error everywhere.

## 3. Zero, 360 and the seam

The scale has no end: 0 degrees is the March equinox and there is no 360. The whole-sky page does have two ends, and where they fall on the scale is the chart time's and the place's, because the page is centred on the ecliptic longitude of the zenith (R-4 of the whole-sky rulings).

| chart time (UTC), from Oslo | longitude at the centre | longitude at both ends | nearest stroke to an end | its distance from the end, px |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2027-12-14 20:00 | 54.97 | 234.97 | 230 | 19.7 |
| 2027-06-15 11:00 | 82.92 | 262.92 | 260 | 11.5 |
| 2027-08-13 00:00 | 15.74 | 195.74 | 200 | 16.9 |
| 2027-12-14 23:00 | 82.84 | 262.84 | 260 | 11.2 |

A stroke exactly at the seam would have to be drawn at both ends or at neither. With a stroke every ten degrees that happens only when the zenith's ecliptic longitude is a whole multiple of ten, which the three-hour step in the last row shows is a matter of minutes of chart time.

## 5. Which longitude: the scale's equinox and the marker's

The atlas's ecliptic is the mean ecliptic of J2000 and its landmarks are J2000's (`docs/decisions/ecliptic.md`); the solar longitude the meteor authorities publish and the atlas evaluates is the Sun's geocentric longitude from the J2000 equinox (R-5 of `docs/decisions/meteor-showers.md`). ## 10. Page units and export

Every size in the mock is a page-pixel constant, as the meridian graduation's stroke (3 either side) and the radiant's rays (3.5 to 10.0) are: a stroke of 3, a figure in the 10 point grid-label font, a caret 7 high on a base of 8. None of them scales with the field. Export draws the page through the one reference layer, as it does the meridian's graduation; a production graduation contributed as geometry would be exported by the same route with no export code of its own.

## 11. What already exists to build on

- **The meridian's graduation (#515)** is the precedent in every respect but one: a tick is furniture, carries no words, is selected by nothing, is in no inventory, takes a clean box for its figure or goes without, and rides its line. Its contribution type states an altitude between 0 and 90, so it cannot carry a longitude as it stands.
- **The ecliptic module has no date** by decision: it has no observer and no instant, and the ecliptic type offers no epoch parameter. A scale in J2000 longitudes needs neither. A marker needs the chart time, which the ecliptic module was built not to have.
- **The solar longitude is already computed and worded**: the meteor panel states it at chart time in both languages ("solar longitude", "sollengde"), and the Sun's own table carries its J2000 ecliptic longitude.
- **The ecliptic's switch is remembered between sessions**; the observer's lines and the meridian's ticks are off at every session start.
