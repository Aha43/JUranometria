## The phase words, proposed

Decided by the illuminated fraction k on the Moon's ruled half-open bands, with two geometric states first:

| word | when |
|---|---|
| in front of the Sun | Mercury's centre within the Sun's apparent radius plus its own, phase angle above 90° (a transit) |
| behind the Sun | the same, phase angle below 90° (Mercury hidden) |
| near inferior conjunction | k < 2 % |
| crescent | 2 % ≤ k < 48 % |
| near half lit | 48 % ≤ k < 52 % |
| gibbous | 52 % ≤ k < 98 % |
| near full | k ≥ 98 % |

The lit side is χ of date, as the Moon's, with the nearest of sixteen points, and *not well-defined* by the same two
thresholds that name the phase near inferior conjunction and near full.

ascension of date (the evening or the morning sky).

### C. The bright limb on the J2000 chart

χ is a position angle from north through east, in the frame its two directions are given in. The table states
χ of date, as the Moon's does; the named table above shows the J2000 χ, from the astrometric ICRF Sun and Mercury,
differing from it by up to the precession of the equator since 2000. A J2000 page must not draw χ of date as if it
were J2000: the Moon's ruling has the later drawing rotate χ into the chart's north basis, not recompute it.
On a page, north is moreover the projection's local north at Mercury's place, not the page's top: the renderer
turns the J2000 direction into the page by the projected direction of increasing declination there, as Jupiter's
pole was derived in the chart's frame (#484).
