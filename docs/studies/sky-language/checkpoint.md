# Ready for owner testing — the Norwegian sky

Issue #347. Seven matched pages and an omission index, for the one
question no measurement answers: **does this read as the familiar
sky?**

Generated by `juranometria.tool.SkyLanguagePairMain` from the
committed name list. Nothing here changes what the application does;
these are study images.

## What you are looking at

Seven sheets, `pair-*.png`. Latin on the left, Norwegian Bokmål on
the right, at the same centre, field, options, size and white-paper
palette. Each half is drawn **independently** through the production
renderer and copied in at native resolution — the generator checks
pixel for pixel that placing a page in a sheet did not alter it, and
the headings sit in a band above the images so nothing about the
comparison touches what is compared.

Each page is drawn at **the limiting magnitude its rung arrives
with**, which the sheet states in its own title block. The globes are
V 5.0, not V 8.0: a reader zooming out to a globe arrives at V 5.0
because zooming out never adds stars, and the earlier drafts of this
study measured a disc a reader does not normally see.

`omissions.png` holds **all ten** names written in one language and
refused in the other — drawn from all fourteen study pages, not only
these seven, because an absence is invisible on a full page by
definition. Each is a native-scale crop of the same place in both
languages, captioned with the page, the identity, the language that
lost it and the refusal that decided it.

## The checklist

Only things an eye can decide. The numbers are in `placement.md`.

1. **Do the Norwegian names feel like the familiar sky?** Not whether
   they are correct — that is settled by provenance — but whether
   they read as a sky rather than as a translation of one.
2. **Are moved names still unmistakably attached?** 102 placement
   observations moved to a different candidate. On the sheets, does
   each still read as belonging to its own figure?
3. **Do the ten omissions leave honest space, or obvious holes?** All
   ten are at a globe's limb. Seven are lost in Norwegian and three
   in Latin, so this is not simply a cost of the longer language.
4. **Is `Slangen` readable as one constellation around Ophiuchus?**
   Serpens is one constellation in two parts with Ophiuchus between
   them. Watch also that the Latin side says `SERPENS CAPUT`, which
   is the catalogue defect #348 will correct: the Norwegian side
   already reads `SLANGEN`, the whole constellation.
5. **Does `Sørlige vannslange` stay comfortable at the globe rim?**
   Eighteen characters against `Hydrus`'s six, on a southern
   constellation that lands where there is least room.
6. **Does Norwegian change the page's quietness or hierarchy?** The
   atlas is meant to be quiet. Do the longer names make it louder, or
   pull the eye away from the stars?

## What is not being asked

Whether the names are right — that is the provenance record's job,
and it is settled: a pinned Wikipedia revision under CC BY-SA, fifteen
forms reviewed individually against Store norske leksikon, every
choice enumerated.

Whether the measurements are right — `placement.md` holds those, with
its own premises and mutations.

This asks only what a person can see and a test cannot.
