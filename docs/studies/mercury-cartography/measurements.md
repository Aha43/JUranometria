# Mercury on the page, and the closer fields: measurements and study pages

Sprint 50, issue #539. What drawing Mercury honestly needs, measured through the production projections, the production renderer and the production bodies layer, with Mercury offered to that layer by this study as a body built from the accepted Mercury service (#529) exactly as the Solar System module builds Venus's: its solid-body disc from the equatorial 2440.53 km, phased by k, χ of date and i, under **rule T** (true scale always, the Sun's and the Moon's C1) or **rule M** (the larger of the true disc and Venus's phased 6 px mark, #512 ruling 1). No production Mercury layer exists: the page's words for Mercury (*Mercury* / *Merkur*, its symbol note and its two states) are this study's proposal, supplied around the production words. The closer fields of #481 are real pages - the same renderer over the same catalogue the 1° page queried, through viewports the ladder does not offer; the ladder is untouched. The readability criterion (a disc of at least 6 px, its lit and dark parts each at least 1 px along the bright limb's axis) is the handovers' study measure, not a rendering rule (#528, ruling 8). Every page says STUDY in its corner. Regenerate with `make mercury-cartography-study`.

## E. The lit shape on the J2000 page

The lit region is the page's own (`ReferenceInk.litRegion`, C3): the half disc towards the bright limb with the terminator's half-ellipse of semi-axis r·|cos i|, turned to χ of date through the page's north and east at Mercury (`PageBasis`), never recomputed from J2000 inputs. With Mercury near the page's edge the basis turns from the page's top by up to 0.055° on the 0.25° page, 0.22° on the 1° page and 1.74° on the 8° page (every 13th week over the century); the 0.25° page turns least, as a narrower gnomonic page must. `MercuryCartographyTest` holds the round trip on the candidate pages: Mercury's J2000 place projects to the page and back to within a microarcsecond, the bodies layer centres the mark on that pixel, and the lit area is k of the disc's.

`closer-fields.png` cuts a 240 px window around the body from each of these pages, at the page's own pixels - no magnification: what each field shows. The findings on the pages themselves, beyond the body:

- **The page's own words rounded the field.** As measured for #539, the title block and the spoken description printed one decimal: the 0.25° page said *Field 0.3°*. A closer field needs the field printed to its own precision. *Met by #543:* these pages now say 0.25 (the table's last column).
- **The grid's finest steps were built for the ladder.** As measured for #539, its smallest steps were 1m of right ascension and 15′ of declination; at 0.25° one step spanned the whole page, so at most one line each way was drawn, often none - the reader lost the grid's scale exactly where scale matters most. A closer field needs finer steps (15s, 2′) before it is offered. *Met by #543:* the table's grid column shows the steps these pages now take.
- **The page may hold no star at all.** A 0.25° field near the ecliptic often contains no catalogue star at V 8.0; the body then stands alone, which is honest but leaves the reader nothing to orient by except the body and the title.
- **The catalogue query is the 1° page's.** The study reuses it, which is exact (the narrower page is inside it); production would query its own field. *Met by #543:* production does, proven in `docs/studies/closer-fields`.
