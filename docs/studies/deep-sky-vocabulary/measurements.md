## Exactly one family per drawable type

A family is defined as one production symbol, so the mapping is a function by construction. What could still go wrong is two families claiming one symbol, or a symbol the renderer draws that no family names - a type that would appear on the chart with no control and no explanation. Both are checked here and again in `DeepSkyVocabularyTest`.

| chart symbol | families naming it | source types drawn with it |
|---|---:|---|
| ellipse | 1 (Galaxies) | G, GPair, GTrpl, GGroup |

**The planetary nebula is the exception**, and it is the renderer's own doing: its four spokes reach 1.7 times its nominal radius, so it draws almost twice the box every other family draws. A legend row that reserved the size it asked for would clip the chart's own symbol. The other four overrun the asked-for size by a pixel, which is the one-pixel outline stroke.

The mock-up draws its symbols at 11 px on a 22 px chip. The widest ink at that size is 20 px (the planetary nebula), so the chip holds every family with 2 px to spare.

## The chart's ink on a dialog's ground

WCAG contrast ratios for the chart's own ink. **Direct** paints the symbol onto the dialog's panel colour. **On paper** paints it onto a scrap of the chart's own white paper, which is what the mock-up does. **Faded** is the same chip at 45% while its family is switched off - the treatment this gate rejects. 3:1 is the WCAG floor for a graphical object.

| theme | panel | ink | direct | on paper | faded to 45% |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| light | #f2f2f2 | outline grey 102 (all but nebulae) | 5.13:1 | 5.74:1 | 1.97:1 |
| light | #f2f2f2 | nebula grey 150 | 2.64:1 | 2.96:1 | 1.56:1 |
| dark | #3c3f41 | outline grey 102 (all but nebulae) | 1.85:1 | 5.74:1 | 2.66:1 |
| dark | #3c3f41 | nebula grey 150 | 3.59:1 | 2.96:1 | 1.92:1 |

Three things follow, and only the third is a choice this gate is free to make:

1. **The symbol must sit on paper.** In the dark theme the chart's outline grey scores 1.85:1 straight onto the panel - invisible - and 5.74:1 on the chart's own white. The chip is not decoration; it is the only way a dark dialog can show the chart's ink at all.
2. **The nebula box is the chart's weakest mark**, at 2.96:1 even on paper. That is the chart's existing palette, not the legend's: the legend shows what the page draws, and changing the chart's ink would change the released rendering. It is recorded as a residual risk rather than quietly corrected in the legend.
3. **A switched-off family does not fade its symbol.** Fading the chip to 45% drops it to 1.97:1 and 1.56:1 - below the floor, in the very state where a reader consults the legend to decide what to switch back on. The checkbox and its text take the platform's disabled styling; the symbol stays fully drawn, because it is information rather than a control.

- `deep-sky-tab-narrow` — at the dialog's narrowest useful width

- `deep-sky-tab-master-off` — master off: the families are remembered, not erased
- `deep-sky-tab-master-off-dark` — master off, dark

- `deep-sky-tab-focus-dark` — keyboard focus, dark

**No two controls on one tab share a letter**, which is the collision that would matter: a mnemonic only reaches the tab in front.

Shared across tabs, harmlessly: F: Flamsteed numbers and Constellation figures. This pair collides **today**, in the single panel of the 1.2.0 dialog, where both controls are visible at once. Separating them onto different tabs is what makes each letter unambiguous - an inherited defect the tabs happen to fix, recorded here rather than left to be discovered.

A mnemonic reaches only the controls on the tab in front, which is a property of tabs rather than of this design: pressing a family's letter while the Stars tab shows does nothing. Tabs are therefore reached the way tabs are reached - Control-Page Up and Control-Page Down, or the arrow keys once a tab has focus - and no meaning anywhere depends on the pointer.
